Research

I work on making physics-informed machine learning reliable enough to trust on PDE problems. My work follows three threads: understanding why PINN training fails on interface problems and fixing it through architecture and loss balancing; learning solution operators from the governing equations alone, without reference solutions; and testing whether neural surrogates reproduce the engineering decisions a verified solver would make. The applications are level-set interface advection, a core problem in multiphase CFD, and heat and moisture transport through building walls in hot-dry climates.

Training PINNs for moving interfaces

A systematic study of PINN design choices for level-set advection, and SDF-Aware Weighting, an adaptive eikonal weight that replaces a manual sweep over four orders of magnitude in 3D.

A Systematic Study of Physics-Informed Neural Networks for the Level-Set Interface Advection

Muhammad Akbar Khan and Fahim Raees

Machine Learning: Science and Technology (IOP Publishing), 2026. Open access, CC BY 4.0

A 69-experiment ablation of PINNs for the level-set advection equation on four benchmarks: linear translation, solid-body rotation, reversed vortex deformation and Zalesak's slotted disc. Eikonal weight tuning reduces error by up to 82×, and the study identifies a joint design constraint between random Fourier feature embeddings and the eikonal weight. A standard tanh network reaches 0.63% relative error on the reversed vortex (T = 8) and 0.13% on Zalesak's disc, lower than reported for PirateNet (Mullins et al., 2025).

A Physics-Informed Neural Operator for Thermal Ranking of Low-Cost Wall Materials in Hot-Dry Climates

Muhammad Akbar Khan, Fahim Raees and Ubaida Fatima

Scientific Reports (Springer Nature), submitted July 2026

A two-stage framework: a Crank–Nicolson finite-difference solver under diurnal solar forcing, and a PINO/FNO surrogate for parametric thermal analysis of five indigenous Sindh wall materials over a nine-dimensional parameter space (1500 Latin hypercube samples). The PINO reaches 0.201 K mean absolute error on peak inner-surface temperature and preserves the solver's material ranking exactly. Trained on 150 samples it matches a data-only FNO trained on 300, and it reproduces the ISO 13786 time lag and decrement factor to within 0.99 h and 0.010.

SDF-Aware Weighting: Adaptive Eikonal Regularisation for Three-Dimensional Level-Set Physics-Informed Neural Networks

Muhammad Akbar Khan

Machine Learning: Science and Technology (IOP Publishing), submitted August 2026

Standard gradient-norm loss balancing fails for eikonal regularisation: the correct level-set solution has a nonzero eikonal residual wherever it departs from a signed-distance function, so driving that residual to zero corrupts the solution. SDF-Aware Weighting (SAW) combines a residual-quantile gate with a gradient-norm ratio, excluding points with legitimate departure before scaling the term. On four 3D benchmarks a single SAW configuration picks an eikonal weight within an order of magnitude of the best in an 18-run sweep spanning 10−1 to 10−5, and on the Zalesak slotted sphere it beats every weight in that sweep. The paper also introduces feature-restricted metrics (Ffill, Ferode) that measure thin-feature preservation separately from global error.

A Fourier Neural Operator Surrogate for Hygrothermal Ranking of Indigenous Wall Materials in Hot-Dry Climates

Fahim Raees and Muhammad Akbar Khan

Machine Learning: Science and Technology (IOP Publishing), submitted September 2026

Tests whether a neural operator reproduces the decision a solver would make, using coupled heat and moisture transport through five indigenous earthen wall materials over a 30-day hot-dry summer. A verified finite-volume solver generates 2000 solutions over a 13-dimensional parameter space, and the FNO predicts temperature and moisture fields to relative L2 errors of 8.7×10−4 and 8.1×10−3. For the scalar ranking quantity a Gaussian process is five times more accurate, yet both surrogates reproduce every solver ranking. Hygric parameters have negligible influence, so the ranking is governed by thermal properties.

A Data-Free Physics-Informed Neural Operator for Level-Set Interface Advection

Muhammad Akbar Khan

arXiv preprint, 2026

A spacetime Fourier neural operator that maps an initial interface to the full level-set trajectory, trained only on the transport residual and an eikonal constraint, with the initial condition imposed by construction and no reference solutions at any point. On a reversed single vortex it reaches 1.61% relative L2 error, against 0.37% for a supervised baseline with the same architecture. Where the eikonal constraint holds it conserves enclosed area 2.7× better than the supervised operator, and a hybrid using eight reference solutions beats a supervised operator using sixteen.
